JTAGulator

Open-source hardware that answers the first question of every hardware hack: where are the debug pins?

Write-up

Modern hardware almost always ships with a debug or programming interface — JTAG, SWD, UART — but manufacturers rarely label it, and it may not be populated with a header. Joe built the JTAGulator to automate the hunt: attach it to a row of candidate test points and it brute-forces combinations, checking for valid JTAG IDCODEs and live UART traffic, then tells you exactly which pins matter.

Released in 2013 and shown at Black Hat USA Arsenal 2014, the design is fully open source (OSHW) — schematics, firmware, and enclosure files included — and it became standard kit in hardware security labs worldwide. Its success is a big part of why "find the debug interface" is now step one of every hardware-hacking class, including his own.

Key specs

  • 24 independently adjustable I/O channels for probing unknown pinouts
  • Adjustable I/O voltage to match target logic levels
  • Detects JTAG chains (IDCODE) and UART connections with baud estimation
  • Completely open source — hardware, firmware, and software
  • Community accessories and clones documented across the ecosystem
