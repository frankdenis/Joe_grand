STM32 fault injection

Open-source glitching research: repeatable voltage-fault workflows for STM32 microcontrollers — the technique family behind the $2M wallet recovery.

Write-up

STM32 parts sit inside a huge share of embedded devices — including first-generation hardware crypto wallets. This research repository collects Joe's fault-injection work: glitch parameters, wiring notes, firmware dumps, and analysis showing how brief voltage disturbances can nudge an STM32 into misbehavior — historically downgrading read protection (RDP level 2 to 1) and exposing flash contents for recovery work.

Everything is published so other researchers can reproduce results on hardware they own: setup photographs, scripts, and measured traces, alongside pointers to the tooling (e.g., ChipWhisperer-class gear, crowbars, and controlled power). If you want the story version, the Trezor recovery write-up shows this technique applied end-to-end on a real locked wallet.

What's inside the repo

  • Glitching setup notes, wiring, and target preparation
  • Scripts and captured traces for parameter sweeps
  • Documented outcomes against read-protection states (for recovery on your own devices)
  • Links to related publications and talks

Published for research and recovery on devices you own or are authorized to assess.
