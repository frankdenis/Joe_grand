The $2M Trezor recovery

A locked wallet, old firmware, one shot. The hack that turned hardware wallet recovery into a business.

How I hacked a hardware crypto wallet and recovered $2 million

6M+ views · covered by The Verge (Jan 24, 2022)

▶

Write-up

In 2021 a friend group reached out after losing access to roughly $2 million in THETA held on a Trezor One — no PIN, no backup of the recovery seed. Joe's earlier research showed that certain STM32-based devices momentarily copy secrets into RAM during firmware updates; a carefully timed voltage glitch could force the chip to expose them.

The recovery took about three months of experimentation: X-raying and characterizing the device, building a glitching rig, reproducing existing research, then executing a single high-stakes run on the real wallet. The glitch worked — PIN and seed appeared on screen, and the funds were recovered. Trezor noted the specific weakness had been patched in 2017 after responsible disclosure, and that the attack requires full physical access — which is exactly why this scenario only happens in a lab, by consent, for owners locked out of their own devices.

The story is why Joe co-founded offspec.io in 2021: legitimate recovery work for people who have lost access to their own hardware and software wallets.
