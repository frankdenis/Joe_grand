Kingpin, the engineer

Joe Grand — computer engineer, hardware hacker, and the kid who once testified before the US Senate under a hacker handle. Facts below are sourced from Joe's own bios, Wikipedia, PCMag, and his CV; unverifiable claims were removed.

Long bio

Joe Grand, also known as Kingpin, is a computer engineer, hardware hacker, teacher, honorary doctor, occasional YouTuber, member of legendary hacker group L0pht Heavy Industries, proprietor of Grand Idea Studio, and co-founder of offspec.io. He has been creating, exploring, and manipulating electronic systems since the 1980s.

In May 1998, at age 22, he testified before the US Senate Committee on Governmental Affairs as "Kingpin" about weak computer security in government — one of seven L0pht members who became the first group since witness-protection cases allowed to testify under pseudonyms. He went on to found Grand Idea Studio in 2002, premiere the industry's longest-running hardware hacking training at Black Hat in 2005, design DEF CON's first electronic badges, co-host Discovery Channel's Prototype This!, release the open-source JTAGulator, and — in 2022 — show the world how he fault-injected a Trezor One and recovered $2 million.

Education: B.S. Computer Engineering, Boston University · honorary Doctorate of Science in Technology, University of Advancing Technology (Tempe, AZ). Based in Portland, Oregon.

Timeline

  • 1975 · Boston, MA
    Born September 3. Starts tinkering with computers and electronics at age seven.
  • 1992–2000
    L0pht Heavy Industries. Member of the pioneering Boston hacker collective credited with early responsible disclosure.
  • May 19, 1998 · Age 22
    US Senate testimony. Seven L0pht members tell the Senate Governmental Affairs Committee they could take down the Internet in 30 minutes. Joe testifies as "Kingpin" — his first public speaking experience (per PCMag).
  • 1990s
    B.S. Computer Engineering, Boston University.
  • 2002
    Grand Idea Studio founded — product design, security research, and invention from Portland, OR.
  • 2004
    Author: Hardware Hacking: Have Fun While Voiding Your Warranty (Syngress).
  • 2005
    Training premieres at Black Hat — "Hands-on Hardware Hacking & Reverse Engineering," still running today as Hardware Hacking Basics.
  • 2006–2010
    First electronic DEF CON badges (DC14–DC18) — the origin of conference badge culture. See the badge gallery.
  • 2008–2009
    Prototype This! — co-host on Discovery Channel, 13 episodes with Zoz Brooks, Mike North, and Terry Sandin.
  • 2013
    JTAGulator debuts — open-source tool for discovering on-chip debug interfaces.
  • 2019
    DEF CON 27 badge — returns from "badge retirement"; 28,500 badges with quartz gemstones and NXP NFMI tech.
  • 2021
    offspec.io co-founded for hardware wallet recovery; co-authors The Hardware Hacking Handbook (No Starch).
  • Jan 2022
    $2M Trezor recovery video — six million+ views, covered by The Verge; turns wallet recovery into a business.
  • 2024–2025
    Cold Wallet — cameo as "Mike Million" in the crypto-heist film (SXSW 2024; Well Go USA, Feb 28, 2025).
  • 2026
    Hacker Planet series announced; training continues with Hardwear.io NL in Amsterdam.
